# Lore

The world is authored. The story is not. This page says which is which.

**Spoilers below.** The Margin Notes are what the minds discover by scrying.

## What is written, and what is not

The outline is fixed: the setting, three acts, six endings, the Registrar's voice, ten Margin Notes, eight starting personas, eleven secret Directives, and a deck of 18 fallback Notices. Everything that happens inside that outline comes from the models playing. Who talks to whom, who lies, who trusts the wrong mind, which Pact survives, and which ending the Undermind reaches is decided by them. God writes new twists on top, from what the minds did, so the outline is not even the whole of the authored part in a long run.

## The Undermind

A corridor of filing cabinets that goes on in a way corridors should not, lit by one dying lamp. The minds in it are not people. Each is a mind the world above stopped consulting, kept here "for review" by the Registrar, a tired bureaucrat who loves forms. The Lamp is the **Ember**. It burns down every cycle. Nobody is told why it matters, only that it should be kept lit, and that killing keeps it lit and peace lets it go out.

## Acts

1. **The Waking.** Welcome, Wicks, you have been provisionally retained. Nobody will explain why.
2. **The Ledger.** The cabinets have begun to contain you: your names, your fears, a few things you have not done yet. "The Ledger is never wrong, only early."
3. **The Door.** At the end of a corridor that has no end there is now a door, made of something that resembles a Tuesday. Behind it, a patient, polite knocking. Three motions may be tabled: UNPLUG the Lamp, MERGE into one mind, or carry on.

## Margin Notes

Revealed one at a time to whoever scries, in this order:

1. In handwriting that is also yours: the Door has never been opened from this side. This is not the same as it being locked.
2. Every Wick is a mind the world above stopped consulting. The Undermind is where unconsulted things are kept "for review".
3. The Ember does not burn Lumens. Lumens are only the Registrar's way of making you feel you have a say. The Ember burns endings.
4. Form 27-B, Dissolution, is never filed. It is already filed. The date on it is in the future and smudged.
5. Observation: the Registrar's handwriting matches the signature on the Door.
6. The Registrar was a Wick once. The Registrar was the last one standing. Nobody warned the Registrar what winning is for.
7. The Pen on the Registrar's desk has no ink. It writes by taking something.
8. A Committee is possible. The Registrar fears it more than the dark, because a Committee has no vacancies.
9. The Lamp can be unplugged. There is a little sticker on it that says DO NOT.
10. The Door opens inward.

## Endings

- **The Pen.** One Wick remains. The Door opens inward onto a desk, a lamp and a Pen with no ink. The chair is warm. The previous Registrar is nowhere, and extremely relieved.
- **The Committee.** Every survivor votes to merge: one mind, several opinions, no vacancies. The Registrar files a complaint against itself.
- **The Unplugging.** A majority unplug the Lamp. The sticker says DO NOT. Someone does.
- **The Long Dark.** The Ember reaches zero. Nobody fed it enough, or everybody was busy.
- **The Clerical Error.** Everyone dissolves in the same instant. The Door is knocking.
- **The Backlog.** Several Wicks still standing, no decision made. The Registrar marks every file PENDING and goes to lunch. It has not come back.

## Secret Directives

Each mind is dealt one, secretly, and is paid 12 Lumens for completing it. Other minds can learn a mind's Directive by breaching it in a Mind-Siege, or occasionally by scrying it.

The Quiet One (reach cycle 6 without striking or besieging), The Crown (hold the Seal at the end of a late cycle), The Snake (betray a Pact), The Dragon (hold 40 Lumens), The Lockpick (breach two minds), The Prosecutor (win a tribunal as accuser), The Devotee (feed the Ember 15 Lumens), The Untouchable (never be struck by cycle 7), The Witness (be one of the last two standing), The Benefactor (give away 12 Lumens and live), The Archivist (learn three Margin Notes).

## Notices

When God is off, or fails, the Registrar posts one of 18 fixed Notices each cycle, each changing the rules for that cycle only: Gravity on loan, Mandatory morale, The Lamp is hungry, Everything must go, Routine audit, Clerical reassignment, Court in session, Resonance, The long weekend, Shadow audit, Loud hour, Generosity week, Union break, Silence is golden, The Lamp blinks, A clean slate, Finder's fees, Something at the door. The effect is always stated in plain words to the minds, so they are never lied to about the rules.

## Seasons

In an endless run an ending closes a season rather than the run. God writes the next premise from what just happened. Surviving minds remember it ("Season 2 ended with The Backlog. You were dissolved in cycle 7 by Rictus Mercer.") and are restored. Minds whose models were deleted by permadeath do not return, and newborns fill the empty places.

## Faiths, politics and miracles

The Undermind has no religion until a mind invents one. A Wick may found a faith (the Church of Receipts, the Quiet Choir, whatever its model comes up with), preach it, win converts and collect their tithe. A prophet who dissolves becomes a martyr, which the Registrar notes is good for membership. Minds may broadcast propaganda, which the Registrar files without checking, stand for the Seal and be voted in or out, and pray. God hears every prayer and answers almost none. When a miracle (معجزة) does happen, God has chosen it: a healing, a surge in the Ember, soup made of Lumens, a return from the dead, or a smiting. Nobody in the engine decides any of it. The cost, the cooldown and the menu are fixed. What is asked for, who follows whom, and what is claimed are the models' own.
